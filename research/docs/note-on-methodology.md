# Note on Methodology

*How albums are chosen for A Jazz Canon. Written 2026-10-20; figures as of that date.*

A Jazz Canon is a personal, curated collection of jazz albums recorded between 1945 and 1985. This note describes how records are selected, because the collection's premise — one listener's judgment applied to tens of thousands of candidate albums — deserves a stated method.

The pool. The LP era of jazz produced on the order of 25,000 to 40,000 original albums worldwide between 1945 and 1985. (The figure is an inference built on observed anchors: Tom Lord's Jazz Discography indexes approximately 456,000 jazz record releases across all eras and formats, and an accounting of the major American jazz labels' catalogs corroborates the order of magnitude.) The canon thus represents roughly one record in a hundred; the method exists to make that hundredth defensible.

The division of labor. Selection proceeds in three strictly separated lanes. An automated nightly process proposes two candidates per day and prepares a complete evidentiary dossier for each: full personnel, recording dates and locations, production credits, and a source map in which every factual claim carries a citation. A panel of independent AI models then evaluates each dossier against a written rubric and issues a formal ballot: a tier, a case for, a case against, and any disagreement among the judges. The pipeline is structurally incapable of advancing an album beyond candidacy. Only the curator admits records, and only after listening to them.

The evidence standard. Every claim in the underlying database carries one of three labels: *obs* (a named source states it), *inf* (reasoned from pattern across sources), or *unk* (sources conflict or are silent). Conflicts between reputable sources are preserved rather than resolved, and precision is never invented: an undocumented recording location is recorded as unknown rather than guessed. The families of sources consulted include the standard session discographies, the major release databases, reference works such as the Penguin Guide to Jazz, the albums' own liner notes, and the critical-list tradition, each cited individually. Rejected candidates are retained with their dossiers and ballots, so the archive records what was weighed as well as what was chosen.

Publication. Additions are exported to the public site at the curator's direction rather than on a schedule. Every status change in the life of an album — candidacy, inclusion, approval, publication — is written to an append-only audit log that carries the deciding words verbatim.

The canon is personal, and says so. The rubric's test is whether a record is the one you would hand someone to explain this music. The method guarantees the integrity of the evidence and the process; the taste remains human.

*As of October 2026 the canon comprises 297 published albums, supported by 1,193 distinct sources and 1,421 individual citations.*
