_**The Immortal Charlie Parker**_ is an [LP record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LP_record "LP record") by saxophonist [Charlie Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Parker "Charlie Parker"), released posthumously by [Savoy Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy_Records "Savoy Records"). Several tracks on this album had been previously released on other formats (78 rpm records, 7 inch [EPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play"), and 10 inch LPs), but is the first 12-inch release of these master takes. It contains selections from five sessions recorded between 1944 and 1948 and also several previously unreleased alternate takes from these sessions.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Immortal_Charlie_Parker#cite_note-liner_notes-1)

## Background

[Charlie Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Parker "Charlie Parker") recorded seven studio sessions for [Savoy Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoy_Records "Savoy Records") between 1944 and 1948; five as a leader and two as a sideman. Twenty-nine tracks from these sessions were released by Savoy on 78 rpm records. Twenty-seven of these tracks were reissued on 7 inch [EPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended_play "Extended play") and 10 inch [LPs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LP_record "LP record") under Parker's name (the other two tracks featuring vocals by guitarist [Tiny Grimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Grimes "Tiny Grimes")). The longer playing 12 inch LP became popular in the mid-1950s and Savoy inaugurated its 12-inch LP series with two albums (this album, and _[Charlie Parker Memorial, Vol. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Parker_Memorial,_Vol._1 "Charlie Parker Memorial, Vol. 1")_ \[Savoy MG 12000\]) which reissued previously released master takes along with previously unreleased alternate takes. Savoy released three more 12 inch albums— _[Charlie Parker Memorial, Vol. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Parker_Memorial,_Vol._2 "Charlie Parker Memorial, Vol. 2")_ \[Savoy MG 12009\], _[The Genius of Charlie Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Genius_of_Charlie_Parker "The Genius of Charlie Parker")_ \[Savoy MG 12014\], and _[The Charlie Parker Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Charlie_Parker_Story "The Charlie Parker Story")_ \[Savoy MG 12079\]—completing the reissue of the previously released master takes, and the original issue of most of the unreleased takes from these seven sessions. Parker's entire Savoy oeuvre was finally issued on _Charlie Parker: The Complete Savoy Studio Sessions_ in 1978.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Immortal_Charlie_Parker#cite_note-jazzdisco-2)

Guitarist [Tiny Grimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiny_Grimes "Tiny Grimes") was the leader of the September 14, 1944 session, which produced the songs "Tiny's Tempo" and "Red Cross". This was also Parker's first recording session for the Savoy label.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Immortal_Charlie_Parker#cite_note-savoy_78-3)

[Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis "Miles Davis") was the leader of the August 14, 1947 session which produced the songs " [Milestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestones_(instrumental_composition) "Milestones (instrumental composition)")", "Little Willie Leaps", "Sipping at Bells", and " [Half Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half_Nelson_(composition) "Half Nelson (composition)")". Davis also wrote all the songs for this session and persuaded Parker to play [tenor saxophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenor_saxophone "Tenor saxophone") instead of his usual [alto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alto_saxophone "Alto saxophone").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Immortal_Charlie_Parker#cite_note-complete_notes-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Immortal_Charlie_Parker#cite_note-birdlives-5)
