_**Art Blakey and the Jazz Messengers**_, also called _**Moanin'**_, is a studio album by [Art Blakey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Blakey "Art Blakey") and [the Jazz Messengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jazz_Messengers "The Jazz Messengers") recorded on October 30, 1958, and released on [Blue Note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Note_Records "Blue Note Records") later that year.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moanin'#cite_note-2)[\[a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moanin'#cite_note-fn1-4)

## Background

This was Blakey's first album for [Blue Note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Note_Records "Blue Note Records") in several years, after a period of recording for a number of different labels, and marked both a homecoming and a fresh start. Originally the [LP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinyl_record "Vinyl record") was self-titled, but the instant popularity of the [bluesy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues") opening track, " [Moanin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moanin'_(song) "Moanin' (song)")," composed by pianist [Bobby Timmons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Timmons "Bobby Timmons"), led to its becoming known by that title.

### Composition

The rest of the originals are by saxophonist [Benny Golson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Golson "Benny Golson") (who was not with the Jazz Messengers for long, this being the only U.S. album on which he is featured). "Are You Real?" is a propulsive 32-bar piece with a four-bar tag, featuring two-part writing for Golson and trumpeter [Lee Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Morgan "Lee Morgan"). "Along Came Betty" is a more lyrical, long-lined piece, almost serving as the album's ballad. "The Drum Thunder Suite" is a feature for Blakey, in three movements: "Drum Thunder"; "Cry a Blue Tear"; and "Harlem's Disciples". " [Blues March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues_March "Blues March")" calls on the feeling of the [New Orleans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans "New Orleans") marching bands, and the album finishes on its only [standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_standard "Jazz standard"), an unusually brisk reading of " [Come Rain or Come Shine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Come_Rain_or_Come_Shine "Come Rain or Come Shine")". Of the originals on the album, all but the "Drum Thunder Suite" became staples of the Messengers book, even after Timmons and Golson were gone. Recorded by [Rudy Van Gelder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudy_Van_Gelder "Rudy Van Gelder") in his meticulous [Hackensack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hackensack,_New_Jersey "Hackensack, New Jersey") studios, this recording reflects the hallmark precision associated with that engineer – on the 1999 CD reissue there is a brief conversation between Lee Morgan and Rudy Van Gelder going over Morgan's solo.

### Influence

A [vocalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocalese "Vocalese") version of "Moanin'" was later written by [Jon Hendricks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Hendricks "Jon Hendricks") and recorded by his group [Lambert, Hendricks & Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambert,_Hendricks_&_Ross "Lambert, Hendricks & Ross") Hendricks sang the song on the 1973 Prestige Records album "Buhaina" (Art Blakey and the Jazz Messengers).[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moanin'#cite_note-5) Other versions include jazz vocalists [Mel Tormé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Torm%C3%A9 "Mel Tormé"), [Bill Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Henderson_(performer) "Bill Henderson (performer)")[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moanin'#cite_note-6) and [Karrin Allyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karrin_Allyson "Karrin Allyson").

Professional ratings

The album stands as one of the archetypal [hard bop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_bop "Hard bop") albums of the era, for the intensity of Blakey's drumming and the work of Morgan, Golson, and Timmons, and for its combination of old-fashioned [gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel_music "Gospel music") and blues influences with a sophisticated modern jazz sensibility. The album was identified by jazz critic [Scott Yanow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Yanow "Scott Yanow") as one of "17 Essential Hard Bop Recordings".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moanin'#cite_note-11) [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic") gives it a five-star review, stating: " _Moanin'_ includes some of the greatest music Blakey produced in the studio with arguably his very best band. ... ranks with the very best of Blakey and what modern jazz offered in the late '50s and beyond."[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moanin'#cite_note-AllMusic-7)

_**Chet Baker Sings**_ is the debut vocal [album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Album "Album") by [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz") musician [Chet Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker "Chet Baker"), released in 1954 by [Pacific Jazz Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Jazz_Records "Pacific Jazz Records"). In 2001, the album received the [Grammy Hall of Fame Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Hall_of_Fame_Award "Grammy Hall of Fame Award").[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-4) Baker would return to selections from this album throughout his career. "My Funny Valentine" was regularly included in his concert performances and is considered by some to be his signature song.

## Background

Until this album, Baker was almost exclusively an instrumentalist. His mother had loved his singing voice and asked him for years to sing more. He eventually sang on two tracks in October 1953: "I Fall in Love Too Easily" and "The Thrill is Gone". Baker then requested to record a full album of his singing, to the skepticism of his producer [Dick Bock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Bock_(producer) "Richard Bock (producer)").[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGavin201184%E2%80%9385-5)

He recorded this album in Hollywood. [Russ Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russ_Freeman_(pianist) "Russ Freeman (pianist)") selected several of the songs for Baker to sing. According to James Gavin, Bock was alarmed as Baker struggled to sing in key, and he reportedly had to make numerous takes on every song. Despite this, Baker remained persistent, to the chagrin of the background musicians. Freeman later said, "I thought it was bullshit. [Louis Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Armstrong "Louis Armstrong") and [Jack Teagarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Teagarden "Jack Teagarden") sang like jazz singers. This was ballads. Totally meaningless."[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGavin201185%E2%80%9386-6)

Baker's later wife [Ruth Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Young_(singer) "Ruth Young (singer)") said of his singing:[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGavin201186-7)

> None of these songs had any meaning for him, truly. He could have been singing [Charmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charmin "Charmin") commercials. He was coming from a musical place, and the words were mere notes to him.

## Critical reception

The reception towards the album, and Baker's singing voice in general, was highly polarized around the album's release.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGavin201186%E2%80%9387-10)

Critics called his voice "fey" and "effete". A biographer for Baker described his voice as "androgynously sweet" and "fragile".[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGavin201186%E2%80%9387-10) The perception of his voice conflicted with the social standards of the era, where gender roles were rigid and homophobia was rampant. It also conflicted with the macho stereotypes of male jazz singers, with examples of [Billy Eckstine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Eckstine "Billy Eckstine") and [Louis Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Armstrong "Louis Armstrong"). Baker later said in 1973:[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGavin201186%E2%80%9387-10)

> "There was a very mixed reaction when I started singing. In the first place, a lot of people thought — foolishly so — that because of the way I sang, I, y'know, _liked fellars_\[ _[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic "Sic")_\] or something. I can only say that that's a lot of bullshit."

According to James Gavin, Baker tried to counteract this by emphasizing his masculinity. He talked frequently about cars and the women in his life and was known to use the homophobic slur " [faggot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faggot "Faggot")".[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGavin201187-11) Despite Baker's intentions, according to James Gavin, the album made Baker the first jazz musician with a "strong homosexual following". The singer-songwriter [Cherry Vanilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_Vanilla "Cherry Vanilla") claimed the album was a hit in the [gay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay "Gay") community. She said "All the gay boys who were into jazz at all were into him."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGavin201186%E2%80%9387-10)

Some critics commented on Baker's singing ability, regardless of its perceived gendered qualities. In _[Metronome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metronome_(magazine) "Metronome (magazine)")_, [Mimi Clar Melnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimi_Clar_Melnick "Mimi Clar Melnick") criticized Baker's "abysmal vocal deficiencies" and compared his singing to "a four-month-old baby's lack of coordination because he can't walk".[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGavin201187-11)

Despite these criticisms, the album was a financial success. In 1956, Bock expanded the album to 14 tracks and a 12 inch rerelease. The additional six tracks were recorded at the Forum Theatre in Los Angeles. The only returning musician on the recording was Russ Freeman; Jimmy Bond played bass, Peter Littman and Lawrence Marable played the drums.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-:1-9)

It was also a significant boost to Baker's fame. It was highly popular among women; Dick Bock conducted a [Pacific Jazz Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Jazz_Records "Pacific Jazz Records") marketing survey in 1955 and found that most of Baker's fans were women who wanted to hear him sing.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGavin2011107%E2%80%93108-12) In the year of the album's release, audience polls in _[DownBeat Magazine's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DownBeat "DownBeat")_ named him the best trumpeter and best vocalist, above [Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis "Miles Davis") and [Nat King Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nat_King_Cole "Nat King Cole") respectively. Afterwards, [Charlie Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Parker "Charlie Parker") told Davis and [Dizzy Gillespie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizzy_Gillespie "Dizzy Gillespie"), "You better watch out... There's a little white cat out in California that's gonna eat you up."[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-:0-13) Soon afterwards, Baker was cast for a part in the film _[Hell's Horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hell's_Horizon "Hell's Horizon")_.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGavin2011108-14)

## Legacy

The album has since become Baker's consistent best seller and one of his most famous.[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-15) Its success led to a sequel in the next year, _[Chet Baker Sings and Plays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings_and_Plays "Chet Baker Sings and Plays")_.[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-16)

A number of later musicians have mentioned the album as a source of inspiration. In 2022, singer-songwriter [Amos Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Lee "Amos Lee") made his own cover of "My Funny Valentine" that was directly inspired by the cover on the album and released the covers album [_My Ideal: A Tribute to_ Chet Baker Sings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/My_Ideal:_A_Tribute_to_Chet_Baker_Sings "My Ideal: A Tribute to Chet Baker Sings").[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-17)

It was a significant inspiration to the [bossa nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bossa_nova "Bossa nova") genre of music and Brazilian music as a whole. It inspired [Carlos Lyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Lyra "Carlos Lyra"), [Roberto Menescal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Menescal "Roberto Menescal"), [Nara Leão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_Le%C3%A3o "Nara Leão"), [Oscar Castro-Neves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Castro-Neves "Oscar Castro-Neves"), and [João Gilberto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Gilberto "João Gilberto"). In particular, Gilberto took direct inspiration from Baker's cover of "Like Someone in Love", and produced his own cover of the song. When Baker later performed in Brazil in August 1980, he was reportedly unaware and surprised that his album had made such an impact there.[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-FOOTNOTEGavin2011319-18)

In 2016, it was reported that original 1954 [10" pressings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonograph_record "Phonograph record") and 1956 Pacific Jazz LP pressings of the album sold for between $50 and $200.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chet_Baker_Sings#cite_note-:0-13)
