_**Milestones**_ is a [studio album](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_album "Studio album") by [Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis "Miles Davis"). It was recorded with his "first [great quintet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis_Quintet "Miles Davis Quintet")" (augmented to a sextet with the addition of alto saxophonist [Cannonball Adderley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannonball_Adderley "Cannonball Adderley")) and released in September 1958 by [Columbia Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Records "Columbia Records").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestones_(Miles_Davis_album)#cite_note-7)

## Composition

At this point, Davis was experimenting with [modal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_jazz "Modal jazz") composition and improvisation, in which a key center is abandoned, and players use scale patterns beyond major and minor.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestones_(Miles_Davis_album)#cite_note-8) Tenor saxophonist [John Coltrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Coltrane "John Coltrane")'s return to Davis' group in 1958 coincided with the "modal phase" albums: _Milestones_ and _[Kind of Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kind_of_Blue "Kind of Blue")_ (1959).

Davis plays both trumpet and piano on "Sid's Ahead", a blues which is reminiscent of "Walkin'" that Davis had recorded under the title ["Weirdo"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis,_Volume_3 "Miles Davis, Volume 3") for Blue Note in 1954. He plays trumpet in the ensemble passages and solos on trumpet but moves to the piano to accompany the saxophonists and [Paul Chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Chambers "Paul Chambers")' bass solo in the absence of pianist [Red Garland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Garland "Red Garland"). " [Billy Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Boy "Billy Boy")" is a rhythm section feature that showcases Garland's signature [block chord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Block_chord "Block chord") playing.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestones_(Miles_Davis_album)#cite_note-9)

Davis himself said about the album in his 1989 autobiography, [Miles: The Autobiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles:_The_Autobiography "Miles: The Autobiography"), "...I loved the way the band sounded on this record and I knew that we had something special. Trane and Cannon were really playing their asses off and by then were really used to each other."[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestones_(Miles_Davis_album)#cite_note-10)

## Critical reception

In a five-star review, [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic")'s Thom Jurek called _Milestones_ a classic album with blues material in both [bebop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebop "Bebop") and [post-bop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-bop "Post-bop") veins, as well as the "memorable" title track, which introduced modalism in jazz and defined Davis' subsequent music in the years to follow.[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestones_(Miles_Davis_album)#cite_note-Jurek-3) Andy Hermann of _[PopMatters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PopMatters "PopMatters")_ felt that the album offers more aggressive [swinging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_(jazz_performance_style) "Swing (jazz performance style)") than _Kind of Blue_ and showcases the first session between saxophonists Coltrane and [Cannonball Adderley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannonball_Adderley "Cannonball Adderley"), whose different styles "feed off each other and push each musician to greater heights."[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestones_(Miles_Davis_album)#cite_note-11) Jim Santella of _[All About Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_About_Jazz "All About Jazz")_ said that the quality of the personnel Davis enlisted was "the very best", even though the sextet was short-lived, and that _Milestones_ is "a seminal album that helped shape jazz history."[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestones_(Miles_Davis_album)#cite_note-12)

_[The Penguin Guide to Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Penguin_Guide_to_Jazz "The Penguin Guide to Jazz")_ selected the album as part of its suggested "Core Collection", calling it "one of the very great modern-jazz albums."[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestones_(Miles_Davis_album)#cite_note-Penguin-6)

## Stereo remix and remaster

_Milestones_ was originally released in mono, as well as in electronically re-channeled stereo (also called [pseudo-stereo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereophonic_sound#Pseudo-stereo "Stereophonic sound")).[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestones_(Miles_Davis_album)#cite_note-13) The album was remixed and remastered in stereo for _[The Complete Columbia Recordings of Miles Davis with John Coltrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Complete_Columbia_Recordings_of_Miles_Davis_with_John_Coltrane "The Complete Columbia Recordings of Miles Davis with John Coltrane")_ and, in 2001, reissued in stereo on the Columbia/Legacy label.
