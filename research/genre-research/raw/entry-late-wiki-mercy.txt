" **Mercy, Mercy, Mercy**" is a jazz song written by [Joe Zawinul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Zawinul "Joe Zawinul") in 1966 for [Cannonball Adderley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannonball_Adderley "Cannonball Adderley") and which appears on Adderley's album _[Mercy, Mercy, Mercy! Live at "The Club"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy,_Mercy,_Mercy!_Live_at_%22The_Club%22 "Mercy, Mercy, Mercy! Live at \"The Club\"")_. The song is the title track of the album and became a surprise hit in February 1967.[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy,_Mercy,_Mercy#cite_note-1) "Mercy, Mercy, Mercy" went to no. 2 on the Soul chart and no. 11 on the [_Billboard_ Hot 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_Hot_100 "Billboard Hot 100") chart.[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy,_Mercy,_Mercy#cite_note-2)

## Original version

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mercy,_Mercy,_Mercy&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Original version")\]

The original version was performed by: Cannonball Adderley (alto saxophone), [Nat Adderley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nat_Adderley "Nat Adderley") (cornet), [Joe Zawinul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Zawinul "Joe Zawinul") (piano, electric piano), [Victor Gaskin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gaskin "Victor Gaskin") (bass) and [Roy McCurdy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_McCurdy "Roy McCurdy") (drums). The theme of the song is performed by Zawinul on a [Wurlitzer electric piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wurlitzer_electric_piano "Wurlitzer electric piano") previously used by [Ray Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Charles "Ray Charles").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy,_Mercy,_Mercy#cite_note-3)

## Musical analysis

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mercy,_Mercy,_Mercy&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Musical analysis")\]

The first part of the theme is played twice and is completely made of notes from the [major pentatonic scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_pentatonic_scale "Major pentatonic scale") of the first degree.

The tune is in the key of B-flat major and has a 20-bar structure with four distinct sections. The chord progression is mainly made of dominant-seventh chords on the first, fourth and fifth degrees, giving the song a bluesy feeling although it does not follow a typical [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues "Blues") progression. The subdominant (IV) chord in the beginning section emphasizes this bluesy feeling. In the second section, the tonic chord alternates with a second-inversion subdominant chord, creating a parallel to the [I-IV-V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-bar_blues "Twelve-bar blues") progression (in which the tonic moves to the subdominant).

## Marlena Shaw cover

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mercy,_Mercy,_Mercy&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Marlena Shaw cover")\]

[Marlena Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marlena_Shaw "Marlena Shaw") recorded a version which peaked at no. 58 on the _Billboard_ Hot 100 on the week of April 1, 1967.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy,_Mercy,_Mercy#cite_note-4)[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy,_Mercy,_Mercy#cite_note-5) Shaw's version had lyrics by [Gail Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gail_Fisher "Gail Fisher") and Vincent Levy, credited as "G. Levy" and "V. Levy." (Fisher's husband at the time was named [John Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Levy "John Levy").) It also peaked at no. 66 on the _Cash Box_ Top 100 Singles chart on the week ending April 8.[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy,_Mercy,_Mercy#cite_note-6)[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercy,_Mercy,_Mercy#cite_note-7)

"Mercy, Mercy, Mercy" has been re-recorded numerous times, most notably by [the Buckinghams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Buckinghams "The Buckinghams"), who reached no. 5 in August 1967, adding lyrics to the tune which were written by Johnny “Guitar” Watson & Larry Williams in Feb, 1967. Musicians on the Buckinghams' version included James Henderson, Lew McCreary and Richard Leith on trombone, Bill Peterson, Bud Childers on trumpet, John Johnson on sax, [Lincoln Mayorga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Mayorga "Lincoln Mayorga") on Wurlitzer electric piano, [Dennis Budimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Budimir "Dennis Budimir") on guitar, [Carol Kaye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carol_Kaye "Carol Kaye") on bass, and [John Guerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Guerin "John Guerin") on drums.\[ _[citation needed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")_\]
