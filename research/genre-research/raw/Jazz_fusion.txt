**Jazz fusion** (also known as **jazz rock**, **jazz-rock fusion**, or simply **fusion**[\[2\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-2)) is a [popular music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_music "Popular music") [genre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_genre "Music genre") that developed in the late 1960s when musicians combined [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz "Jazz") harmony and [improvisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_improvisation "Jazz improvisation") with [rock music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_music "Rock music"), [funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk "Funk"), and [rhythm and blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues "Rhythm and blues"). [Electric guitars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_guitar "Electric guitar") and [basses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_guitar "Bass guitar"), [amplifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar_amplifier "Guitar amplifier"), and [keyboard instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyboard_instrument "Keyboard instrument") (including [electric pianos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_piano "Electric piano") and [organs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_organ "Electric organ")) that were popular in rock began to be used by jazz musicians, particularly those who had grown up listening to rock and roll.

Jazz fusion [arrangements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrangements "Arrangements") vary in complexity. Some employ groove-based vamps fixed to a single key or a single chord with a simple, repeated melody. Others use elaborate [chord progressions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chord_progression "Chord progression"), unconventional time signatures, or melodies with [counter-melodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauptstimme "Hauptstimme"), in a similar fashion to [progressive rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_rock "Progressive rock"). These arrangements, whether simple or complex, typically include improvised sections that can vary in length, much like in other forms of jazz.

As with jazz, jazz fusion can employ brass and [woodwind instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodwind_instrument "Woodwind instrument") such as trumpet and saxophone, but other instruments often substitute for these. A jazz fusion band is less likely to use acoustic [piano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piano "Piano") and [double bass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_bass "Double bass"), and more likely to use electric guitars, electric pianos, organs, [synthesizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthesizer "Synthesizer"), and [bass guitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bass_guitar "Bass guitar").

The term "jazz rock" is sometimes used as a synonym for "jazz fusion" and for music performed by late 1960s- and 1970s-era rock bands that added jazz elements to their music. After a decade of popularity during the 1970s, fusion expanded its improvisatory and experimental approaches through the 1980s in parallel with the development of a radio-friendly style called [smooth jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smooth_jazz "Smooth jazz").[\[3\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-3) Experimentation continued in the 1990s and 2000s. Fusion albums, even those that are made by the same group or artist, may include a variety of musical styles. Rather than being a codified musical style, fusion can be viewed as a musical tradition or approach.

### Origins

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jazz_fusion&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Origins")\]

When [John Coltrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Coltrane "John Coltrane") died in 1967, rock was the most popular music in America, and _[DownBeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DownBeat "DownBeat")_ magazine went so far as to declare in a headline that: "Jazz as We Know It Is Dead".[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Fusions-4) [AllMusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AllMusic "AllMusic") states that "until around 1967, the worlds of jazz and rock were nearly completely separate",[\[5\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Fusion_overview-5) although the [Daevid Allen Trio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_Machine "Soft Machine") which later became the Soft Machine formed in 1963,[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-6) while the jazz label [Verve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verve_Records "Verve Records") released the influential debut album, _[Freak Out!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freak_Out! "Freak Out!")_, by artist and composer [Frank Zappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Zappa "Frank Zappa") in 1966.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-OxMilkowski-7)

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/34/Larry_Coryell_1979.jpg/250px-Larry_Coryell_1979.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Larry_Coryell_1979.jpg) Guitarist [Larry Coryell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Coryell "Larry Coryell")

Guitarist [Larry Coryell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Coryell "Larry Coryell"), sometimes called the godfather of fusion, referred to a generation of musicians who had grown up on rock and roll when he said, "We loved Miles but we also loved the [Rolling Stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stones "Rolling Stones")."[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-OxMilkowski-7) In 1966, he started the band [the Free Spirits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Free_Spirits "The Free Spirits") with [Bob Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Moses_(musician) "Bob Moses (musician)") on drums and recorded the band's first album,[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Fusions-4) _[Out of Sight and Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_of_Sight_and_Sound "Out of Sight and Sound")_, released in 1967. That same year, _DownBeat_ began to report on rock music.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-OxMilkowski-7) After the Free Spirits, Coryell was part of a quartet led by vibraphonist [Gary Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Burton "Gary Burton"), releasing the album _[Duster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duster_(Gary_Burton_album) "Duster (Gary Burton album)")_ with its rock guitar influence.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Fusions-4) Burton produced the album _Tomorrow Never Knows_ for Count's Jam Band, which included Coryell, [Mike Nock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Nock "Mike Nock"), and [Steve Marcus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Marcus "Steve Marcus"), all of them former students at Berklee College in Boston.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Fusions-4)

The pioneers of fusion emphasized exploration, energy, electricity, intensity, virtuosity, and volume. [Charles Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Lloyd_(jazz_musician) "Charles Lloyd (jazz musician)") played a combination of rock and jazz at the [Monterey Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monterey_Jazz_Festival "Monterey Jazz Festival") in 1966 with a quartet that included [Keith Jarrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Jarrett "Keith Jarrett") and [Jack DeJohnette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_DeJohnette "Jack DeJohnette").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-OxMilkowski-7) Lloyd adopted the trappings of the California [psychedelic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychedelic_rock "Psychedelic rock") scene by playing at the rock venue the [Fillmore West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fillmore_West "Fillmore West"), wearing colorful clothes, and giving his albums titles like _Dream Weaver_ and _Forest Flower_, which were bestselling jazz albums in 1967.[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Fusions-4) Flautist [Jeremy Steig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Steig "Jeremy Steig") experimented with jazz in his band Jeremy & the Satyrs with vibraphonist [Mike Mainieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Mainieri "Mike Mainieri"). [Rahsaan Roland Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahsaan_Roland_Kirk "Rahsaan Roland Kirk") performed with Jimi Hendrix at [Ronnie Scott's Jazz Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronnie_Scott's_Jazz_Club "Ronnie Scott's Jazz Club") in London.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-OxMilkowski-7)

According to music journalist Zaid Mudhaffer, the term "jazz fusion" was coined in a review of _[Song of Innocence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_of_Innocence "Song of Innocence")_ by [David Axelrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Axelrod_(musician) "David Axelrod (musician)") when it was released in 1968.[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-8) Axelrod said Davis had played the album before conceiving _[Bitches Brew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitches_Brew "Bitches Brew")_.[\[9\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-9)

### Miles Davis

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jazz_fusion&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Miles Davis")\]

[Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis "Miles Davis") wrote in his autobiography that in 1968 and 1969, he had been listening to [Jimi Hendrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimi_Hendrix "Jimi Hendrix"), [James Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Brown "James Brown"), and [Sly and the Family Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sly_and_the_Family_Stone "Sly and the Family Stone").[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-OxMilkowski-7) As members of Davis' band, [Chick Corea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chick_Corea "Chick Corea") and [Herbie Hancock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock "Herbie Hancock") played electric piano on _[Filles de Kilimanjaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filles_de_Kilimanjaro "Filles de Kilimanjaro")_. Davis's 1969 album _[In a Silent Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_a_Silent_Way "In a Silent Way")_ is considered his first fusion album.[\[10\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Southall-10) Composed of two side-long improvised suites edited heavily by Teo Macero, the album was made by pioneers of jazz fusion: Corea, Hancock, [Tony Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Williams_(drummer) "Tony Williams (drummer)"), [Wayne Shorter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Shorter "Wayne Shorter"), [Joe Zawinul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Zawinul "Joe Zawinul") and [John McLaughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McLaughlin_(musician) "John McLaughlin (musician)"). When Davis recorded _[Bitches Brew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitches_Brew "Bitches Brew")_ in 1969, he mostly abandoned the swing beat in favor of a rock and roll backbeat and bass guitar grooves. The album "mixed free jazz blowing by a large ensemble with electronic keyboards and guitar, plus a dense mix of percussion".[\[11\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-11) Davis played his trumpet like an electric guitar—plugged in to electronic effects and pedals. By the end of the first year, _Bitches Brew_ sold 400,000 copies, four times the average for a Miles Davis album.

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/81/Miles_Davis_24.jpg/250px-Miles_Davis_24.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Miles_Davis_24.jpg) Trumpet player [Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis "Miles Davis") was a key figure in the development of fusion.

Although _Bitches Brew_ gave him a [gold record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_recording_sales_certification "Music recording sales certification"), the use of electric instruments and rock beats created consternation among some jazz critics, who accused Davis of betraying the essence of jazz.[\[12\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-considine-12) Music critic Kevin Fellezs commented that some members of the jazz community regarded rock music as less sophisticated and more commercial than jazz.[\[13\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Briley-13) Over the next two years, Davis recorded more often, worked with many sidemen, appeared on television, and performed at rock venues, continuing to experiment with rock, [funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funk "Funk"), and electronic treatments. His producer, [Teo Macero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teo_Macero "Teo Macero"), inserted previously recorded material into the [_Jack Johnson_ soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Johnson_(album) "Jack Johnson (album)"), _[Live-Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live-Evil_(Miles_Davis_album) "Live-Evil (Miles Davis album)")_, and _[On the Corner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Corner "On the Corner")_.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Gioia_history-14) _Jack Johnson_ (1971) has been cited as "the purest electric jazz record ever made" and "one of the most remarkable jazz rock discs of the era".[\[15\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-tribute-15)[\[16\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Fordham_tribute-16)

### Other musicians

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Jazz_fusion&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Other musicians")\]

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/75/JohnMcLaughlin.jpg/250px-JohnMcLaughlin.jpg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:JohnMcLaughlin.jpg) [John McLaughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McLaughlin_(musician) "John McLaughlin (musician)") performs during his [Mahavishnu Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahavishnu_Orchestra "Mahavishnu Orchestra") period

Herbie Hancock brought elements of funk, disco, and electronic music into commercially successful albums such as _[Head Hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_Hunters "Head Hunters")_ (1973) and _[Feets, Don't Fail Me Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feets,_Don't_Fail_Me_Now "Feets, Don't Fail Me Now")_ (1979). Several years after recording _[Miles in the Sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_in_the_Sky "Miles in the Sky")_ with Davis, guitarist [George Benson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Benson "George Benson") became a vocalist with enough pop hits to overshadow his earlier career in jazz.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Gioia_history-14)

While Davis was sidelined, Chick Corea gained prominence. In the early 1970s Corea combined jazz, rock, pop, and Brazilian music in [Return to Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_to_Forever "Return to Forever"), a band that included [Stanley Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Clarke "Stanley Clarke") on bass guitar and [Al Di Meola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Di_Meola "Al Di Meola") on electric guitar. Corea divided the rest of his career between acoustic and electric music, non-commercial and commercial, jazz and pop rock, with a band for each: the Akoustic Band and the Elektric Band.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Gioia_history-14)

Joe Zawinul and Wayne Shorter started the influential jazz fusion band [Weather Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_Report "Weather Report") in December 1970.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-BKGrove-17) They had a successful career along with major musicians like [Alphonse Mouzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphonse_Mouzon "Alphonse Mouzon"), [Jaco Pastorius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaco_Pastorius "Jaco Pastorius"), [Airto Moreira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airto_Moreira "Airto Moreira") and [Miroslav Vitouš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miroslav_Vitou%C5%A1 "Miroslav Vitouš") until 1986.[\[17\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-BKGrove-17)

Tony Williams was a member of Davis's band since 1963. Williams reflected, "I wanted to create a different atmosphere from the one I had been in...What better way to do it than to go electric?" He left Davis to form [the Tony Williams Lifetime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tony_Williams_Lifetime "The Tony Williams Lifetime") with English guitarist John McLaughlin and organist [Larry Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Young_(musician) "Larry Young (musician)"). The band combined rock intensity and loudness with jazz spontaneity. The debut album [_Emergency!_](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency!_(album) "Emergency! (album)") was recorded three months before _Bitches Brew_.[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-OxMilkowski-7)[\[18\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-18)[\[19\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-19)

Although McLaughlin had worked with Miles Davis, he was influenced more by Jimi Hendrix and had played with English rock musicians [Eric Clapton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Clapton "Eric Clapton") and [Mick Jagger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Jagger "Mick Jagger") before creating the Mahavishnu Orchestra around the same time that Corea started Return to Forever. McLaughlin had been a member of Tony Williams's Lifetime. He brought to his music many of the elements that interested other musicians in the 1960s and early 1970s: counterculture, rock and roll, electronic instruments, solo virtuosity, experimentation, the blending of genres, and an interest in the exotic, such as Indian music.[\[14\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-Gioia_history-14) He formed the [Mahavishnu Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahavishnu_Orchestra "Mahavishnu Orchestra") with drummer [Billy Cobham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Cobham "Billy Cobham"), violinist [Jerry Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Goodman "Jerry Goodman"), bassist [Rick Laird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Laird "Rick Laird"), and keyboardist [Jan Hammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Hammer "Jan Hammer"). The band released its first album, _[The Inner Mounting Flame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Inner_Mounting_Flame "The Inner Mounting Flame")_, in 1971. Hammer pioneered the use of the [Minimoog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimoog "Minimoog") synthesizer with distortion effects. His use of the pitch bend wheel made a keyboard sound like an electric guitar. The Mahavishnu Orchestra was influenced by both psychedelic rock and [Indian classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_music "Indian classical music"). The band's first lineup broke up after two studio albums and one live album, but McLaughlin formed another group in 1974 under the same name with jazz violinist [Jean-Luc Ponty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Luc_Ponty "Jean-Luc Ponty"), one of the first electric violinists.

After leaving the Mahavishnu Orchestra in 1975 Jean-Luc Ponty signed with [Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Records "Atlantic Records") and released number of successful jazz fusion solo albums that entered top 5 of the [_Billboard_ jazz charts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_Jazz_Albums "Top Jazz Albums") in mid '70s — '80s.

During the late 1970s, [Lee Ritenour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Ritenour "Lee Ritenour"), [Stuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuff_(band) "Stuff (band)"), [George Benson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Benson "George Benson"), [Chuck Mangione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Mangione "Chuck Mangione"), [Spyro Gyra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spyro_Gyra "Spyro Gyra"), [the Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crusaders_(jazz_fusion_group) "The Crusaders (jazz fusion group)"), and [Larry Carlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Carlton "Larry Carlton")[\[20\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-20) released fusion albums. Early 1980s, Larsen Feiten band, [George Benson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Benson "George Benson"), [Lee Ritenour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Ritenour "Lee Ritenour"), [Al Jarreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jarreau "Al Jarreau"), and the Clark/Duke Project gained pop hits.[\[21\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_fusion#cite_note-21)
