_**Speak No Evil**_ is the sixth album by [Wayne Shorter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Shorter "Wayne Shorter"). It was released in June 1966 by [Blue Note Records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Note_Records "Blue Note Records").[\[1\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speak_No_Evil_(Wayne_Shorter_album)#cite_note-1) The music combines elements of [hard bop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_bop "Hard bop") and [modal jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_jazz "Modal jazz"), and features Shorter on tenor saxophone, trumpeter [Freddie Hubbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freddie_Hubbard "Freddie Hubbard"), pianist [Herbie Hancock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbie_Hancock "Herbie Hancock"), bassist [Ron Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Carter "Ron Carter") and drummer [Elvin Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvin_Jones "Elvin Jones"). The cover photo is of Shorter's first wife, Teruko (Irene) Nakagami, whom he met in 1961.

Professional ratings

_Speak No Evil_ was one of several albums Shorter recorded for Blue Note in 1964, his first year as a member of [Miles Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Davis "Miles Davis")'s mid-1960s quintet. The album is generally regarded as one of Shorter's finest, and also a highlight of the Blue Note catalogue. _The Penguin Guide to Jazz_ selected this album as part of its suggested "Core Collection" calling it "by far Shorter's most satisfying record".[\[4\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speak_No_Evil_(Wayne_Shorter_album)#cite_note-Penguin-4) [Murray Horwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murray_Horwitz "Murray Horwitz") stated in 2001 that " _Speak No Evil_ is sort of a consolidation of Wayne Shorter's compositional excellence. It's so thorough and consistent and wide-ranging. It's almost a manifesto for his ideas. Those ideas were new 40 years ago, but they're still fresh today."[\[6\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speak_No_Evil_(Wayne_Shorter_album)#cite_note-6) [Rough Guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_Guides "Rough Guides") author [Ian Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Carr "Ian Carr") wrote it is "a classic album in terms of both composition and improvisation, and has been inspirational for many musicians".[\[7\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speak_No_Evil_(Wayne_Shorter_album)#cite_note-Rough_Guide_to_Jazz-7) _[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times "New York Times")_ critic [Ben Ratliff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Ratliff "Ben Ratliff") included the album in his publication _Jazz: A Critic's Guide to the 100 Most Important Recordings_ stating that it offers "the first taste of a gnomic compositional style that would haunt jazz forevermore. \[...\] Just about everybody playing jazz born in the 1950s and after accepts it as a foundation."[\[8\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speak_No_Evil_(Wayne_Shorter_album)#cite_note-Ratliff-8)

## Releases

\[ [edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Speak_No_Evil_(Wayne_Shorter_album)&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Releases")\]

_Speak No Evil_ was initially released on LP in 1966, as BLP 4194 and BST 84194. The first CD release was in 1987. A 1999 CD version, supervised by [Rudy Van Gelder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudy_Van_Gelder "Rudy Van Gelder"), included an alternate take of "Dance Cadaverous". A remastered limited edition was issued in Japan on SHM-CD in 2013, including two previously unissued alternate takes.

In 2015, the original master tapes were remastered and cut in the all-analog domain by Kevin Gray for Music Matters Ltd as part of their Blue Note: The Definitive Vinyl Reissue Series.
