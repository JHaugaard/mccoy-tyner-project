### The two quotations the page's argument rests on

> "Although 'out music' is generally avant-garde, avant-garde music is not necessarily 'out'…
> what is 'outside' to someone may not be 'outside' to another." — Duhaime, *Eric Dolphy & Outness*

> "What's considered outside is subjective and changeable. What you hear as 'outside' someone
> else will hear as 'inside,' and vice versa. Bird was considered 'out' by many musicians in
> the 1940s, as was Coltrane in the 1960s." — Mark Levine, *The Jazz Theory Book*, p. 183,
> quoted in Duhaime

**Handle with care:** both are second-hand (Duhaime quoting Gridley and Levine). If John
wants either on the public page, the primary book should be checked first. They are
converging reports from a scholarly source, which is a reasonable basis for an advisory —
and a weak basis for a printed claim.

## 4. Verification log — things I checked and got wrong, or nearly

Recorded because a research corpus that hides its corrections is not trustworthy.

1. **The canon does contain bebop and outside material.** An early pass read
   `canon-draft.json` (100 albums) and concluded the canon held no bebop, no free jazz, no
   fusion. That was an artefact of reading a stale working draft as the live collection.
   The live export has 5 bebop and 16 outside albums. The draft-based claim was wrong and
   was corrected in `../04-players/canon-inventory.md`.
2. **"Bird on 52nd Street" carries the label "Jazz Workshop" in the export.** This looked
   like a data error. It is not: Concord's own catalogue states the album was made for the
   short-lived Jazz Workshop label. Verified, no action.
3. **Two gateway years were wrong in a first draft of the listener's guide** — *Maiden
   Voyage* is 1965 (not 1964) and *The Sidewinder* is 1963 (not 1964). Corrected against the
   live export.
4. **The Internet Archive text stream for the All Music Guide to Jazz contains mostly
   preface material.** The body of the book is not reliably there. Do not cite that URL as if
   it were the full book.
5. **The canon's modal/hard-bop arrival years in an early timeline draft were off by one**
   (hard bop 1951 vs the computed 1952). Corrected against computed values.

## 5. Open sourcing gaps

If John wants to state any of the following on a public page, they need a primary source
first — I did not establish them this session:

### Corrections to premises that were circulating in this project

**The "dust jacket hypothesis" is NOT a verified, citable scholarly position.** I introduced
it into this project as a live debate to be reported. A subagent went looking for it as a
*named* position and could not find it. What actually exists is narrower:

- Ben Baker's review of Keith Waters concedes the premise — that critics' categories were
  "partially 'marketing' to some degree" — while still defending Waters's category as real
  and useful. A concession of the premise, not an endorsement of the thesis.
  ([MTO 26.3](https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.20.26.3/mto.20.26.3.baker.html))

So: the *critics-invented-the-terms-to-dismiss-the-music* framing is **not established**, and
the page should not state it as a settled scholarly view. If John wants to use it, it needs a
named proponent and a citation.

**Soul Jazz Records founding date is unresolved: 1991 per Wikipedia, 1992 elsewhere.** I
passed 1992 into a research brief; that figure is not confirmed. The page's only real need is
the distinction — a 1990s London reissue label is not the origin of the genre term — which
holds either way.

**I passed two quotes to a research brief as "verified" and they could not be found.** In the
brief for the outside-branches file I supplied, as sourced: James Moore's account of Miles
reacting to hearing Eric Dolphy — "ridiculous," "sad," "bad" — and Thelonious Monk on Ornette
Coleman — "nothing beautiful… playing loud and slurring." A subagent grepped the entire
corpus for both, including `raw/pod-book-cooks-lee.txt`, which is the natural home for the
Monk remark. **Zero hits.** They are widely repeated and sound right, but I have not banked
a source for either.

Both are quarantined in `06-inside-outside-thesis/three-outside-branches-compared.md`, where
its own contested section says to bank the primary or cut them. They appear in **no** other
file in this corpus and **not** in `inside-outside.html`. Treat them as unverified lead
material, not as evidence.

### Evidence sought and not found

- **The "Chamber of Jazz" scare.** No primary evidence of a literal 1960s "Chamber of Jazz"
  scare was located. The closest retrieved material is a January 1961 *Jazz Journal* review
  of Paul Desmond's *Easy Jazz* (Michael Shera) calling it "pleasant, inoffensive, but
  essentially trivial," with an "almost complete absence of swing," plus a CUNY thesis
  noting hard bop fans called the MJQ "too polite." That is real criticism of cool jazz and it
  is enough to make the seam argument — but it is **not** the same as a documented panic, and
  the page should not describe one unless the *Down Beat* / *Metronome* 1959–61 archives are
  searched.
- **Rosenthal, "Hard Bop and Its Critics"** (*Black Perspective in Music* 16:1, 1988). The
  citation is confirmed from search results; the article text was not retrieved. Central to
  the claim that hard bop was defended *against* its own critics — worth obtaining.

### Still open

- The precise origin of the term "bebop," and which date historians now prefer for bebop's birth.
- The *No Jazz* survey (Miller and Briant, late 1970s) — surfaced in search, never read.
- The Jazz Workshop's exact founding date and institutional lineage to the AACM.
- Personnel disputes on the 1945–47 Savoy and Dial bebop sessions.
- Whether the Village Vanguard "New Thing" engagement can be dated, or whether the *Newport*
  programme of 2 July 1965 is the only firm date.
- Bill Evans's "LaMarchal" — the thematic lineage is well founded; the album placement is not verified.
- Gunther Schuller's *Thesaurus of Scales and Melodic Patterns* (1949) and *The Technique of
  Modal Composition* — unverified.

### A source that should be treated with care

**Britannica pages carry AI-generated "Top Questions" panels.** These are not Britannica
editorial voice and should not be quoted as such. Flagged during extraction by a subagent.
