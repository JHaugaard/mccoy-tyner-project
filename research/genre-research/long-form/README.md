# The long-form path

**Audience:** John. This is the research and thinking underneath whatever he eventually
writes, and it is a programme rather than a deliverable — it may not produce a single page.

---

## What this path is not

It is not the explainer with more words. The failure mode to avoid is a "deep version" that
just adds detail to the same genre-by-genre structure. The two paths should diverge early and
stay diverged.

**The most useful long-form material is not in the genres.** Anyone can write a longer
chapter on hard bop. The genuinely hard, genuinely interesting questions live in the
*seams*, the *politics*, and the *people* — and the existing corpus is thinnest exactly
there. That is where this path should go.

---

## The four research fronts, in priority order

### 1. The politics of the categories — the biggest gap
**Nothing in the existing corpus treats this head-on**, and it is arguably the most
important thing on the page for a public reader.

The fragments we have are alarming and pointing somewhere:

- John Tynan's "anti-jazz" attack (*DownBeat*, 1961) is quoted in the corpus but never
  analysed. It is a full-frontal attack on the new music, and it is *about* race as much as
  about music.
- Critics colour-coded the field: cool and Third Stream read as white, hard bop and soul
  read as black. "Respectable" functioned as a racial term.
- Brubeck's *Time Out* was framed as a fight for Black music's "respectability" — Dave
  Brubeck's own framing, retrieved in the corpus (Klotz, *Daedalus*).
- Miles reportedly heard Eric Dolphy and said "ridiculous." Monk on Ornette Coleman:
  "nothing beautiful… playing loud and slurring." **Both unverified in our corpus** — logged
  in `../long-form/sources/sources.md` — and they are the shape of an argument about
  belonging, not about notes.

The question this path should answer: **who got called "outside," who got called a
sellout, and on what grounds?** It connects the aesthetic question to the economic one, and
it is where the term "inside/outside" turns out to have a second, older meaning than the
musical one — established versus marginal, in a market with racial fault lines running
through it. The corpus has this in §7 of the terminology file but it is the least developed
section there, and it should become a front of its own.

### 2. The moving boundary
What "outside" has meant in each era, and how the meaning shifted: musical in the 1940s,
critical in the early 1960s, economic in the 1970s. Starting point: Levine's observation that
Bird was "out" in the 1940s. Ending question: is there a period in which *nothing* was
outside, or is there always a designated fringe? The seam files and the three-branches file
are the raw material.

### 3. The 1950s experimental wing
Treated thinly throughout. The avant-garde/experimental lineage ran *alongside* bebop from
about 1955 and is far older than free jazz — Transition, Contemporary, Globe Unity, George
Russell. It predates the categories the page uses. Understanding it would make the whole
framework more honest, because it shows the boxes were drawn late.

### 4. The straddlers, as a subject
The three-branches file treats the people who cross the lines as evidence that the lines are
porous. That is the right use. But the individuals are worth following on their own terms —
the Miles sidemen dispersing into five different bands, Don Cherry, Kamasi Washington, the
ECM circle. This is where the aesthetic argument becomes a social one.

## Standing constraints

- **The canon is one lens among several, never the frame.** It is a good lens — a single
  person's 254-record selection is a real and unusual perspective — but it is a *selection*.
  A finding is more interesting if something *outside* the canon complicates it.
- **Every claim keeps its uncertainty.** The per-genre files already carry contested sections
  and this path should not launder that away by summarising.
- **Contested stays contested.** The "dust jacket" hypothesis, the Chamber of Jazz scare and
  the two unverified quotes are all currently unbanked. Do not let them harden into fact by
  being repeated.

## Open questions — unanswered, and worth answering

These are the ones the corpus flagged and could not settle. They are the natural backlog.

| Question | Why it matters | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| Who first used "post-bop," and as praise or as dismissal? | The term is central to the canon's taxonomy | *Down Beat* / *Metronome* 1964–70 |
| Did a "chamber jazz" panic actually happen in 1959–61? | A received narrative the corpus cannot support | *Down Beat* / *Metronome* 1959–61 |
| Rosenthal, "Hard Bop and Its Critics" (1988) | Hard bop defended *against* its own critics — text not retrieved | *Black Perspective in Music* 16:1 |
| Miles on Dolphy; Monk on Coleman | Two quotes in wide circulation, no source banked | Interviews, biographies, contemporaneous press |
| Personnel disputes on the 1945–47 Savoy and Dial sessions | Bebop's foundation is partly unrecorded | Sessionography; DeVeaux on the *Parker Story* |
| Can bebop's birth be dated, or is that the wrong question? | The page's earliest genre | DeVeaux; the five competing answers in `genres/bebop.md` |
| What did the term "outsider" cost people careers? | Connects front 1 to front 2 | Musicians' oral histories, 1960s–70s press |

## How work gets done here

This path is the natural consumer of delegated research, and **researcher** is available
again as of 2026-09-29. The pattern that worked in the first pass: research and writing are
separate, and the research stage banks raw captures to disk so that a writing pass is cheap
and does not have to re-derive. Two subagent attempts burned their whole budget on research
and wrote nothing; the third, told to read banked material and write, produced the best
files in the corpus.

Worth keeping that lesson: **budget the writing explicitly, or it will not happen.**
