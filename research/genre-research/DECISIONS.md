## Decision 1 — Does the "outside is a relative term" argument anchor the explainer?

**The question.** Make the relativity of inside/outside the *spine* of the beginner page —
i.e. the thing the page is built around and returns to — or state it once, early, and then
proceed with a more conventional genre-by-genre structure?

**The material.** Mark Levine, *The Jazz Theory Book*: *"What's considered outside is
subjective and changeable… Bird was considered 'out' by many musicians in the 1940s, as was
Coltrane in the 1960s."* The inside tradition was once the radical one. Eric Dolphy, the
figure most often filed as the prime "outside" player, said: *"I think of my playing as
tonal… every note I play has some reference to the chords of the piece."*

**Recommendation: make it the spine.**
- It is the most interesting thing in the entire corpus.
- It is fully sourced, and it survives the canon de-rating.
- It needs no music theory, so it is reachable by a complete beginner.
- It is what earns the page its title, and it is the honest version of the argument.
- It inoculates the reader against thinking the categories are settled.

**The cost, stated honestly.** It makes the page slightly harder to write and slightly less
tidy, because the argument keeps undermining the categories the page is introducing. A
reader who wants a clean map may find it evasive.

**Where it is now.** `explainer/structure.md` Part 1 is written on the assumption that the
answer is yes. If the answer is no, Part 1 shrinks to three sentences and Part 2 needs to
carry the page instead.

## Decision 2 — Split fusion out as "The Electric Turn"?

**The question.** Does fusion become its own section on its own axis, separate from the
free jazz / avant-garde group?

**Why it is being asked.** The original brief grouped free jazz, fusion and avant-garde into
one catch-all. That grouping is historically sloppy and, on the evidence, wrong in the
harmonic sense. Fusion is electric and groove-driven but plays overwhelmingly *inside* the
changes — vamps, riffs, ostinatos, backbeats. *Bitches Brew* is radical in texture, timbre,
studio construction and volume, not in abandoning chord changes.

So there are three axes, not one: **harmonic/formal**, **acoustic/electric**, and
**composed/improvised**. The title names the first. Fusion belongs to the second.

Support: mccoy's independent counsel — *"let fusion stand separately as 'The Electric Turn' —
different axis, different story."* The site's own taxonomy already splits the territory into
Fusion / Jazz-Rock / Jazz-Funk rather than one bucket. My analysis and mccoy's converged
separately.

**Recommendation: yes.**
- Nothing in the original brief is dropped; one item moves to the axis it actually belongs to.
- It stops the page teaching that electric music and free music are the same argument.
- It makes the page more interesting: two different revolutions happening at once, rather
  than one "outside thing."

**The cost.** Eight sections instead of seven, and a slightly more complicated idea for a
beginner. Manageable — the section can open by saying "this is a *different* argument, not a
louder version of the same one."

**Where it is now.** `explainer/structure.md` Part 4 assumes yes.

## Decision 3 — Is the outside one section or two?

**The question.** Free jazz and avant-garde jazz as (a) one section with two clearly marked
subsections, or (b) two separate top-level sections?

**The evidence.** They are genuinely different lineages. In the site's own taxonomy,
avant-garde is the AACM compositional wing and free jazz is the Coleman/Trane/Ayler line.
Mark Gridley's warning is the right frame: the era's loose category names *"led some
outsiders to assume… that the different musicians of this period had more in common than
they actually did."* Merging them is a real and common historical error.

**Recommendation: one section, two clearly marked subsections**, with one sentence stating
plainly that they are not the same thing.
- Two top-level sections over-promises; it implies the distinction is as clean as the
  headings suggest, and it is not.
- One undifferentiated section teaches the error.
- Two marked subsections inside one section is the only option that does neither.

**The cost.** The distinction gets less visual weight than it arguably deserves. If John
prefers clarity over economy, two sections is defensible.

**Where it is now.** `explainer/structure.md` Part 3 assumes one section / two subsections.
